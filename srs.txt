I. Project Introduction

1. Overview
Boxora is a web-based Self-Storage Facility Rental and Management System designed to centralize the rental and operational management of storage facilities. The system supports the rental lifecycle from facility and unit discovery, reservation and payment to contract confirmation, check-in, handover, access management, renewal, overdue handling, checkout, and deposit refund.
The platform is designed for multiple facilities and supports different operational roles, including Storage Customer, Facility Staff, Facility Manager, Business Operations Manager, and System Administrator.

2. Product Background
Self-storage facilities require the management of physical storage units, customer reservations, rental contracts, payments, deposits, access credentials, unit handover, inspections, and contract expiry. When these activities are handled separately, staff must spend significant time checking records, updating unit status, communicating with customers, and reconciling payments.
Boxora addresses these operational requirements by connecting the major activities into one rental lifecycle. Customers can discover facilities and available units, create reservations, make payments, confirm contracts, schedule check-in, manage active rentals, renew contracts, request checkout, and submit support requests. Staff and managers receive operational functions for verification, unit assignment, handover, inspection, overdue handling, and reporting.

3. Existing Systems
3.1 Traditional Manual Self-Storage Management
In a traditional self-storage operation, facility information, unit availability, customer information, contracts, payments, and handover records may be maintained through paper documents, spreadsheets, phone calls, or separate internal records.
This approach can make it difficult to obtain a real-time view of unit availability and rental status. Reservation processing, unit assignment, contract expiry monitoring, payment reconciliation, and checkout inspection also require repeated manual work. Errors or delayed updates can lead to inconsistent records between staff and the actual physical condition or status of a unit.

3.2 Fragmented Digital Storage Management
Some storage operations may use digital tools for individual activities such as customer registration, payment recording, spreadsheets, or communication. However, when these tools are not integrated into a single rental workflow, staff may still need to transfer information between systems or maintain duplicate records.
A fragmented approach can also make facility-scoped authorization, end-to-end rental tracking, overdue processing, access-credential management, support requests, and auditability harder to manage consistently. Boxora therefore focuses on integrating these activities into a centralized platform.

4. Business Opportunity
The self-storage service model creates an operational need for convenient online discovery and reservation as well as reliable facility-side management. Customers need clear information about facility locations, unit types, prices, availability, rental duration, payments, and access. Facility operators need tools to control inventory, contracts, staff activities, overdue cases, revenue, and facility performance.
Boxora provides an opportunity to digitize these processes in one system. The platform can reduce repeated manual operations, improve visibility of storage inventory and rental status, standardize operational workflows, and provide structured data for revenue, occupancy, overdue, and activity reporting.
The system is also designed with multi-facility operation in mind. Facility-scoped authorization allows operational users to work within their assigned facilities while Business Operations Managers and System Administrators can manage information at the appropriate broader scope.

5. Software Product Vision
Boxora aims to provide a centralized and traceable digital platform for self-storage rental operations. The product vision is to make storage rental easier for customers while giving operators a consistent way to manage facilities, storage units, contracts, payments, access, staff operations, and customer support.
For customers, Boxora provides a clear digital journey from searching for a suitable storage unit to reservation, payment, contract confirmation, check-in, active rental, renewal, and checkout. For facility operators, the system provides structured workflows, authorization by facility scope, scheduled processing, operational dashboards, reporting, and audit logging.
The Phase 1 product focuses on software-based rental and operational management. Direct integration with smart-lock hardware, transportation services, online liquidation, and sub-user access sharing are excluded from the MVP scope.

6. Project Scope & Limitations
The project scope covers the core software functions required to manage the self-storage rental lifecycle and supporting facility operations. The system is designed as a web-based application with separate capabilities according to user roles.

6.1 Major Features
1. Customer facility and unit discovery: browse facilities, unit types, dimensions, prices, availability, and relevant facility information.
2. Reservation management: select a facility, unit type, start date and rental duration; create and manage reservations; apply a 24-hour soft-lock for pending reservations.
3. Reservation payment: support deposit and first-rental payments through a sandbox payment provider and reconcile payment results through signed webhooks.
4. Rental contract management: create and confirm rental contracts and maintain contract status and rental information.
5. Check-in and handover: schedule check-in, verify customer information, verify payment and contract eligibility, assign an appropriate physical unit, record handover conditions, and complete the rental start process.
6. Access credential management: manage storage-unit access PINs and credential status according to the active contract and access-control workflow.
7. Rented storage unit management: allow customers to view active rented units, contract information, payment information, expiry dates, and access information.
8. Renewal and overdue management: provide renewal options, reminders, overdue detection, penalties, PIN disabling, sealing workflow, and offline legal-processing stages.
9. Checkout and deposit refund: request checkout, perform inspection, record unit condition and evidence, calculate additional charges, and process the remaining deposit refund.
10. Support request management: allow customers to create support tickets and authorized staff to receive, update, and close them.
11. Facility and staff management: manage facilities, storage units, staff assignments, and facility-level operational information.
12. Pricing, fees, discounts and policies: manage unit pricing, rental policies, fees, discounts, and related business rules.
13. Reporting and dashboards: provide occupancy, revenue, overdue, facility, and system-wide operational reports.
14. User, role and permission management: manage users, roles, permissions, facility assignments, authentication, login history, and audit logs.
15. Scheduled processing and notifications: automate reservation expiry, renewal reminders, overdue detection, and relevant customer notifications.

6.2 Limitations & Exclusions
1. Direct Bluetooth, Zigbee, or remote smart-lock hardware integration is excluded from Phase 1.
2. Third-party home pickup, transportation, or moving services are excluded.
3. Online auction or liquidation of abandoned property is excluded; legal processing remains offline in Phase 1.
4. Sub-user access sharing is excluded from the MVP; access is limited to the contract holder.
5. The payment integration uses a sandbox provider for the project and is not intended to represent a production payment deployment.
6. Physical storage-unit assignment remains a manual authorized operation in the MVP; automatic allocation is deferred.
7. The system does not directly operate physical locks or other facility hardware.

II. Project Management Plan

1. Overview
The Project Management Plan defines how the Boxora team organizes, develops, reviews, tests, documents, and delivers the Self-Storage Facility Rental and Management System. The plan covers project scope, objectives, risks, development process, quality activities, training, deliverables, responsibilities, communication, and configuration management.

1.1 Scope & Estimation
The project includes requirements analysis, system design, database design, backend development, frontend development, integration, testing, documentation, and release preparation. Development is organized around the core rental lifecycle and the supporting management functions defined in the Software Requirements Specification.
The project estimation is divided into the following major work packages: requirements and analysis; UI and system design; database and backend development; frontend development; integration and business workflow implementation; testing and defect fixing; documentation and release preparation.
The team will prioritize Must-have requirements first, followed by Should-have requirements where time and project capacity allow. Changes to the baseline requirements are reviewed by the team before implementation.

1.2 Project Objectives
1. Deliver a working web-based self-storage rental and management system for the defined user roles.
2. Implement the complete core rental lifecycle from discovery and reservation through payment, contract, handover, active rental, renewal, overdue handling, checkout, and deposit refund.
3. Provide role-based and facility-scoped authorization for operational users.
4. Provide reliable payment-status processing, auditability, and traceability for critical business operations.
5. Provide dashboards and reports for facility and system-level operational monitoring.
6. Complete the required software documentation, testing documentation, installation instructions, and user guides.

1.3 Project Risks
- Risk R1 – Requirement changes: Changes to business rules or functional scope may increase development effort and affect the schedule. Mitigation: maintain a requirements baseline and review changes before implementation.
- Risk R2 – Integration issues: Frontend, backend, database, payment sandbox, and notification components may produce integration defects. Mitigation: integrate incrementally and perform API and end-to-end testing.
- Risk R3 – Payment inconsistency: Client-side payment results may differ from gateway callback results. Mitigation: treat signed webhook results as the authoritative reconciliation event and design callback processing to be idempotent.
- Risk R4 – Authorization errors: Incorrect facility-scoped authorization may expose or modify data outside a user's assigned facility. Mitigation: enforce RBAC and facility-scope checks in backend services and test unauthorized scenarios.
- Risk R5 – Data inconsistency: Unit status, reservations, contracts, and payments may become inconsistent if concurrent operations are not handled correctly. Mitigation: validate state transitions, use database constraints/transactions where appropriate, and test boundary cases.
- Risk R6 – Schedule pressure: Limited capstone time may reduce the time available for testing or documentation. Mitigation: prioritize core requirements, track milestones, and reserve time for integration and defect fixing.
- Risk R7 – Hardware dependency: Smart-lock or other physical integrations may introduce additional technical and operational complexity. Mitigation: keep direct hardware control outside Phase 1 and provide software-based access-credential management.

2. Management Approach
Boxora follows an iterative software development approach. Requirements are organized into functional modules, implemented incrementally, integrated with related modules, and verified through testing. The team maintains a shared baseline for requirements, source code, database migrations, and project documentation.

2.1 Project Process
- Phase 1 – Requirements Analysis: identify actors, use cases, business rules, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, and project constraints.
- Phase 2 – System and Database Design: define architecture, modules, entity relationships, database schema, authorization boundaries, and major workflows.
- Phase 3 – Implementation: develop backend APIs, frontend screens, database migrations, authentication, business workflows, and supporting services.
- Phase 4 – Integration: connect frontend and backend modules and verify complete workflows such as reservation-payment-contract-check-in-handover and renewal-overdue-checkout-refund.
- Phase 5 – Testing and Stabilization: execute unit, integration, functional, authorization, and user acceptance testing; record and fix defects.
- Phase 6 – Documentation and Release: finalize the SRS, design documentation, test documentation, installation guide, user manual, and release package.

2.2 Quality Management
Quality is managed through requirement reviews, coding standards, peer review, functional testing, authorization testing, database validation, and regression testing after significant changes.
Critical workflows such as payment reconciliation, unit assignment, handover, overdue processing, checkout, deposit refund, and audit logging receive specific verification because errors in these workflows can affect business records or access control.
The project uses the following quality targets defined for the system: business APIs should meet the documented response-time targets under normal test conditions; critical activities should be auditable; role and facility authorization should be enforced consistently; and the release package should contain the required installation and user documentation.

2.3 Training Plan
Training is provided through the user manual and practical walkthroughs for each supported role:

- Facility Manager training covers facility dashboard usage, unit management, customer and reservation monitoring, overdue cases, staff assignment, and facility reports.
- Business Operations Manager training covers facility management, unit types, pricing, rental policies, fees, discounts, and system-wide reports.
- System Administrator training covers user, role, permission, facility-scope, login-history, and activity-log management.

Boxora is a centralized web-based platform for renting and managing self-storage facilities across multiple locations. It replaces manual reservation, paper-based handover, fragmented payment tracking, manual contract-expiry monitoring and ad-hoc support handling with an integrated digital workflow.
The platform provides a Customer Portal, Facility Staff functions, Facility Manager functions, Business Operations management and System Administration. The system supports facility-scoped authorization, online payment, e-contract confirmation, access credentials, automated renewal reminders, overdue processing, support tickets and operational reporting.

1.1 Product Objectives

3.2 Authentication and User Management
3.2.1 Sign In
- Function Trigger: User submits valid login credentials.
- Actors: All system users
- Purpose: Authenticate the user and establish token-based access.
- Interface: Login form
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve account by email/username.
  - Verify password hash and account status.
  - Issue short-lived access token and refresh-token mechanism.
  - Record login event.
- Function Details: The system denies invalid credentials and inactive accounts. Authorized requests use the user's role and facility scope.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Invalid credentials -> authentication error.
  - Inactive account -> access denied.
  - Repeated failures -> security event recorded.

3.2.2 Manage Users
- Function Trigger: Administrator selects Create, View, Update or Deactivate.
- Actors: System Administrator
- Purpose: Maintain user accounts and role/facility assignments.
- Interface: Table + form/modal
- Data Processing:
  - Validate account fields and uniqueness.
  - Validate role and facility assignment.
  - Persist changes.
  - Create audit record for privileged changes.
- Function Details: Admin can search, filter, create, update, activate/deactivate users and assign roles.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Duplicate email/username -> reject.
  - Invalid role/scope -> reject.
  - Unauthorized user -> 403.

3.3 Facility Management
3.3.1 View Facilities
- Function Trigger: User opens facility catalog.
- Actors: Guest, Customer, Staff, Facility Manager, Ops Manager, Admin
- Purpose: Display facilities and availability.
- Interface: Cards/list + filters
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve facility records.
  - Calculate available-unit counts.
  - Filter by city and unit type.
- Function Details: Read-only catalog showing name, address, contact, status, unit types, prices and availability.

3.3.2 Create / Update Facility
- Function Trigger: Authorized manager submits facility form.
- Actors: Business Operations Manager, System Administrator
- Purpose: Maintain facility master data.
- Interface: Form
- Data Processing:
  - Validate required fields and unique code/name.
  - Validate status transition.
  - Persist facility.
  - Audit change.
- Function Details: A facility can be created, updated or deactivated according to business dependencies.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Invalid data -> validation error.
  - Unsafe deactivation -> reject or require approved handling.

3.4 Unit Type and Pricing Management
3.4.1 Manage Unit Type
- Function Trigger: Authorized user opens Unit Type management.
- Actors: Business Operations Manager, Facility Manager where authorized
- Purpose: Define storage categories, dimensions and base pricing.
- Interface: Table + form
- Data Processing:
  - Validate name, dimensions, area and price.
  - Associate type with facility.
  - Persist configuration.
- Function Details: Typical categories include Small, Medium and Large.

3.4.2 Price History
- Function Trigger: Operations Manager changes a unit-type price.
- Actors: Business Operations Manager
- Purpose: Preserve historical prices and prevent retroactive changes to active contracts.
- Interface: Form + history table
- Data Processing:
  - Validate effective dates and amount.
  - Close/version the prior price period.
  - Store new price history record.
- Function Details: New prices apply to new reservations/contracts. Active contracts retain the monthly fee locked at signing.

3.5 Storage Unit Management
3.5.1 View Storage Units
- Function Trigger: Authorized user opens unit management.
- Actors: Facility Staff, Facility Manager, Ops Manager, Admin
- Purpose: Show physical unit inventory and status.
- Interface: Table / floor-map style view
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve units by facility, type, floor and status.
  - Apply facility authorization.
- Function Details: Typical statuses: Available, Reserved, Occupied, Maintenance, Overdue_Locked, Inactive.

3.5.2 Assign Physical Unit
- Function Trigger: Staff/Manager selects a valid reservation and available unit.
- Actors: Facility Staff, Facility Manager
- Purpose: Assign a physical unit to a reservation.
- Interface: Assignment form / facility map
- Data Processing:
  - Validate reservation status.
  - Validate facility and unit type match.
  - Verify unit availability.
  - Prevent concurrent double assignment.
  - Update reservation and unit status.
  - Record audit event.
- Function Details: MVP uses manual physical-unit assignment before check-in.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Unit unavailable -> 409.
  - Wrong facility/type -> reject.
  - Race condition -> reject conflicting assignment.

3.6 Reservation Management
3.6.1 Create Reservation
- Function Trigger: Customer clicks Reserve after choosing facility, unit type, start date and duration.
- Actors: Storage Customer
- Purpose: Create a temporary reservation and calculate initial charges.
- Interface: Reservation form + price summary
- Data Processing:
  - Validate minimum 1-month duration.
  - Check availability.
  - Calculate deposit and first rental payment.
  - Set 24-hour expiration.
  - Create Pending_Payment reservation.
- Function Details: The reservation temporarily holds inventory and returns reservation code, payment URL and expiry time.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - No availability -> 409.
  - Duration below minimum -> validation error.
  - Concurrent reservation -> reject and refresh availability.

3.6.2 Expire Reservation
- Function Trigger: Scheduled job finds an unpaid reservation after 24 hours.
- Actors: System Scheduler
- Purpose: Release inventory from abandoned reservations.
- Interface: Background job
- Data Processing:
  - Find Pending_Payment reservations where expires_at has passed.
  - Verify no successful payment exists.
  - Mark Expired.
  - Release inventory.
- Function Details: The released unit/capacity becomes available to other customers.

3.7 Payment Management
3.7.1 Create Payment
- Function Trigger: Customer confirms deposit/rental/renewal/extra-fee payment.
- Actors: Storage Customer, Payment Gateway
- Purpose: Initiate an online payment.
- Interface: Payment page / QR / gateway redirect
- Data Processing:
  - Validate payable amount and payment type.
  - Create Pending PaymentTransaction.
  - Send payment request to gateway.
- Function Details: Client-side success is never treated as final payment confirmation.

3.7.2 Payment Webhook
- Function Trigger: Gateway sends a signed webhook.
- Actors: Payment Gateway
- Purpose: Synchronize payment status with Boxora.
- Interface: Backend webhook endpoint
- Data Processing:
  - Verify signature.
  - Verify transaction reference and amount.
  - Process webhook idempotently.
  - Update payment status.
  - Trigger reservation/contract transition.
- Function Details: A successful deposit payment confirms the reservation; required first-rental payment enables handover.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Invalid signature -> 400.
  - Unknown transaction -> 404.
  - Duplicate webhook -> no duplicate financial effect.

3.8 Rental Contract Management
3.8.1 Create and Sign E-Contract
- Function Trigger: Required payment succeeds and contract prerequisites are met.
- Actors: Storage Customer, System
- Purpose: Create the formal rental agreement.
- Interface: Contract page + OTP
- Data Processing:
  - Prepare contract terms.
  - Lock monthly fee.
  - Validate OTP.
  - Store signature metadata and contract.
- Function Details: The contract records start/end dates, unit, deposit and locked monthly fee.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Invalid/expired OTP -> reject.
  - Missing payment prerequisite -> cannot activate.

3.8.2 View Contract
- Function Trigger: Authorized user opens a contract.
- Actors: Customer, Staff, Facility Manager
- Purpose: Display rental terms and status.
- Interface: Detail page
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve contract.
  - Apply ownership or facility-scope authorization.
- Function Details: Sensitive information is shown only to authorized actors.

3.9 Check-in and Handover
3.9.1 Check-in
- Function Trigger: Customer arrives and presents QR/reservation code.
- Actors: Facility Staff, Storage Customer
- Purpose: Verify eligibility before physical handover.
- Interface: Staff check-in screen
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve reservation/customer/unit.
  - Verify full deposit.
  - Verify first rental payment.
  - Verify signed e-contract.
  - Verify original identity document.
  - Verify appointment/date.
- Function Details: The handover action is disabled until all four core prerequisites are satisfied.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Incomplete payment -> block.
  - Missing contract signature -> block.
  - Invalid identity/booking -> block.
  - Wrong appointment -> block or authorized exception.

3.9.2 Handover and Access Credential
- Function Trigger: Staff completes the handover checklist.
- Actors: Facility Staff
- Purpose: Record unit condition and issue access.
- Interface: Handover checklist
- Data Processing:
  - Check cleanliness and lock condition.
  - Record key/card/PIN.
  - Store credential securely.
  - Create handover record.
  - Set contract Active and unit Occupied.
- Function Details: MVP does not directly control smart-lock hardware; PIN/card/key is managed as a controlled credential.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Unit damaged/unready -> notify Manager and reassign an equivalent unit.
  - Missing checklist -> cannot complete handover.

3.10 Rented Storage Unit Management
3.10.1 View My Units
- Function Trigger: Customer opens My Units.
- Actors: Storage Customer
- Purpose: Manage one or more rented units.
- Interface: Cards/list + detail
- Data Processing:
  - Retrieve active contracts and units.
  - Retrieve expiry, payment and access information.
  - Filter to customer-owned rentals.
- Function Details: Customer can view contract, rental period, payment status and access instructions.

3.10.2 Request Checkout
- Function Trigger: Customer submits a return request.
- Actors: Storage Customer
- Purpose: Start the unit-return process.
- Interface: Checkout request form
- Data Processing:
  - Validate contract ownership and state.
  - Create checkout task.
  - Schedule return appointment if applicable.
- Function Details: The task is visible to the assigned facility staff.

3.11 Renewal and Overdue Management
3.11.1 Renew Contract
- Function Trigger: Customer selects Renew while eligible.
- Actors: Storage Customer
- Purpose: Extend rental period and collect renewal payment.
- Interface: Renewal form + payment
- Data Processing:
  - Validate renewal window.
  - Calculate renewal amount.
  - Create payment.
  - On success, extend end date.
- Function Details: Online renewal is available at least 3 days before contract expiry; supported cycles are 1, 3, 6 and 12 months.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Too close to expiry -> online renewal disabled.
  - Payment failure -> contract unchanged.

3.11.2 Overdue Processing
- Function Trigger: Daily scheduler runs at 00:00.
- Actors: System Scheduler, Facility Manager
- Purpose: Detect overdue rentals and apply staged controls.
- Interface: Background job + Manager overdue screen
- Data Processing:
  - Find expired active contracts without valid renewal/payment.
  - At +1 mark Overdue, calculate penalty and notify.
  - At +3 disable PIN.
  - At +7 create sealing task.
  - At +30 trigger offline legal liquidation workflow.
- Function Details: Facility Manager approval is required for physical sealing or security-lock changes.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Authorized exception may temporarily delay access restriction.
  - All privileged actions are audited.

3.12 Checkout, Inspection and Deposit Refund
3.12.1 Checkout Inspection
- Function Trigger: Customer returns the unit and staff opens the checkout task.
- Actors: Facility Staff, Storage Customer
- Purpose: Verify condition and calculate deductions.
- Interface: Responsive checklist
- Data Processing:
  - Complete five-point condition checklist.
  - Capture at least two photos.
  - Record damage/cleaning fees.
  - Calculate refund_estimated_amount = deposit_held - damage_fee - unpaid_overdue_fee.
  - Create handover/checkout record.
- Function Details: If inspection passes, the unit can return to Available after required processing.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Missing checklist/photos -> cannot complete.
  - Damage found -> evidence and fee required.
  - Outstanding overdue -> apply approved deduction.

3.12.2 Deposit Refund Review
- Function Trigger: Checkout inspection is approved.
- Actors: Facility Manager / Finance workflow
- Purpose: Track the refundable deposit.
- Interface: Refund request/detail
- Data Processing:
  - Validate approved deductions.
  - Verify refund calculation.
  - Create Pending_Accounting_Review request.
- Function Details: Remaining deposit is transferred within 3–5 business days according to the baseline policy.

3.13 Support Request and Issue Management
3.13.1 Create Support Ticket
- Function Trigger: Customer submits an issue from a rented unit.
- Actors: Storage Customer
- Purpose: Create a traceable support request.
- Interface: Ticket form
- Data Processing:
  - Validate customer-unit relationship.
  - Capture category, subject, description, priority and optional photos.
  - Associate the ticket with facility.
- Function Details: Initial status is Open and the ticket is routed to the relevant facility.

3.13.2 Handle Support Ticket
- Function Trigger: Staff opens a ticket in their facility scope.
- Actors: Facility Staff, Facility Manager
- Purpose: Track issue resolution.
- Interface: Ticket detail
- Data Processing:
  - Validate facility scope.
  - Assign staff.
  - Update status.
  - Record resolution notes.
  - Record audit event.
- Function Details: Typical lifecycle: Open -> Assigned -> In_Progress -> Resolved -> Closed.
- Alternate / Error Flows:
  - Wrong facility -> 403.
  - Invalid transition -> reject.
  - SLA breach -> flag/escalate.

3.14 Facility Staff Management
3.14.1 Assign Staff
- Function Trigger: Manager/Admin creates or updates staff assignment.
- Actors: Facility Manager, System Administrator
- Purpose: Define facility scope for staff operations.
- Interface: Assignment form
- Data Processing:
  - Validate user role.
  - Validate facility.
  - Validate assignment dates.

- Function Details: Staff and Facility Managers operate only within assigned facility scope.

3.15 Fee, Pricing and Policy Management
3.15.1 Configure Policies
- Function Trigger: Operations Manager opens policy configuration.

- Purpose: Centralize rental, deposit, cancellation, renewal, overdue and extra-fee rules.

- Data Processing:

3.15.2 Calculate Charge

- Actors: System, Storage Customer
- Purpose: Produce a transparent payable amount.
- Interface: Price summary
- Data Processing:

- Function Details: The finalized amount is stored with the relevant reservation, contract or payment transaction.

7. Baseline Decisions and Constraints
- MVP uses a shared database with facility_id as the data-scope discriminator and application-level facility authorization.
- Physical storage-unit assignment is manual in MVP; auto-assignment is deferred.
- E-contract is confirmed by OTP before physical handover.
- MVP does not directly control smart-lock hardware.
- Payment integration uses a sandbox provider and signed webhook reconciliation.
- Minimum rental is one month; renewal cycles are 1, 3, 6 and 12 months.
- Sub-user access sharing is excluded from MVP.
- Deposit refunds follow an accounting review workflow with a 3–5 business-day SLA.
